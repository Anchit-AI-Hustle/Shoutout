CH 01

Brand architecture

One name, one primitive, every surface. The reason SHOUTOUT works is structural, not sentimental.

The primitiveA shoutout is a call that someone answers. That single object — a Shout — is the atomic unit of the entire platform. A producer shouts for a vocalist. An organiser shouts for a headliner. A season shouts for talent. A brand shouts for an artist. And when the platform shouts about you, that is recognition. Collaboration, booking, auditions and sponsorship are not four products. They are four kinds of Shout on one rail.

Trademark strategy — the honest position

"Shoutout" is common English vernacular. That is a real weakness and it does not go away by wishing. It is managed, not solved, and here is how:

MoveReason
File SHOUTOUT as one word, no article, always styled"The ShoutOut" is weaker still and breaks every domain and handle. One word, all caps, one lockup.
File the device mark before the word markThe stylised logotype is where your defensible rights actually live. Protect the shape, then argue the word.
Class 41 first, then 9, 42, 35, 36Class 41 (entertainment, live events, TV formats) is where your use is most distinctive and least crowded. Establish there, extend outward.
Own the verb in-product"Post a Shout." "Answer a Shout." Consistent proprietary usage over time builds secondary meaning, which is what turns a weak mark into a defensible one.
Register sub-brands as separate marksSHOUTOUT STAGE, SHOUTOUT STUDIO, SHOUTOUT CREDITS. Compound marks are meaningfully more registrable than the bare word.
Domain convention: shoutout.fm primary.fm is the music TLD convention and reads as intentional rather than as a compromise. Secure .app, .live, .music, and getshoutout.com defensively.
Accept this trade knowinglyYou are trading trademark strength for brand-story coherence and cultural warmth. That is a defensible trade — Stripe, Slack and Square all began with common words — but it means budgeting for a longer, more expensive path to a defensible mark, and never letting a competitor use "shout" as a product noun unchallenged.

Verbal identity

  • The verb is the brand. "Put out a shout." Never "create a listing," never "post a request."
  • Artist-side, never gatekeeper. The platform works for the person posting, not above them.
  • Numerate where money is involved. Fees, splits, deductions and payout timing stated plainly and early. Warmth in the culture surfaces, precision in the money surfaces.
  • Bilingual by default in India. Hinglish and code-switching in the app and on social; clean global English in investor and brand material.
  • Recognition is the emotional core. Every state change that means someone got picked — matched, booked, selected, paid — is a moment the product should mark, not just log.

CH 05

Collab network

A producer needs a vocalist. Or a songwriter and a vocalist, independently, on the same track. This is the module that makes SHOUTOUT a network rather than a booking tool.

Why this changes the companyGig booking is transactional — a manager returns when there is a date to fill. Collaboration is daily. A producer opens the app to find a topliner on a Tuesday afternoon with no gig in sight. That shifts SHOUTOUT from a system of record to a system of habit, which is the difference between a tool people keep and a tool people forget.

The multi-role Shout — worked example

SHOUT posted by @kalmi_beats (role: beat producer) title "Hindi R&B — need topline + vocal" tempo 92 BPM key A minor mood nocturnal, restrained attached beat_v3.mp3 + reference generated in Studio SLOT 1 songwriter · Hindi · R&B terms: 15% writer split SLOT 2 vocalist · female · Hindi terms: ₹12,000 flat + 5% deadline 14 days visibility open / invite-only / roster-only MATCHING each slot matched independently against its own role pool ANSWERS applicants attach a 30-second demo against the actual beat CLOSE producer picks per slot → split sheet auto-drafted → all three sign → escrow funds slot 2 → stems released → on delivery: splits register to SHOUTOUT Credits

The point: slots are independent. One Shout can fill a writer and a vocalist from two different people, with different terms — cash for one, points for the other — and the split sheet reconciles all of it before a single file changes hands.

Split sheet at session time — the non-negotiable rule

No stems are released until every slot's split is agreed and signed. This is the single most important design decision in the module. The industry's oldest, most corrosive problem is undocumented splits discovered years later when money finally arrives. SHOUTOUT makes the split a precondition of the file transfer, not an afterthought — and every agreed split writes directly into the credits registry, which is how the registry gets populated without anyone doing data entry.

Trust & safety

  • Watermarked previews. Beats and stems stream watermarked until the deal closes. Full-quality files unlock on signature.
  • Reliability score per role: delivered on time, revisions honoured, splits upheld, disputes raised against them. Visible, and it decays if inactive.
  • Verified identity before any paid Shout — phone plus PAN or equivalent. Free collabs can stay lighter-weight.
  • Work-for-hire vs co-write declared on every slot up front. Ambiguity here is where relationships die.
  • Dispute path that freezes escrow and preserves version history as evidence, rather than leaving two people arguing in DMs.

CH 07

Flows & state machine

The two flows that define the company: a booking from brief to re-book, and an audition from submission to onboarding.

Hold ledger — state machine

enquiry→soft_hold_1→soft_hold_2→soft_hold_3→confirmed→contracted→advance_paid→performed→settled→closed
exits:releasedcancelleddisputedexpired

Rules that make it work: Every soft hold carries a mandatory release deadline. First hold has right of first refusal; when a second party wants the date, hold 1 is challenged and has 48 hours to confirm or drop. Auto-release fires from a scheduled job, not a user action. confirmed cannot be entered without a contract draft attached. settled cannot be entered until every split — manager, agency, sidemen — is reconciled to zero.

Two blockers to clear before any code ships1. Escrow is not legally yours to hold. Under RBI payment-aggregator rules you cannot hold customer funds. Use a licensed PA's split-settlement product with hold periods — functionally escrow, legally theirs. The nodal account sits with the PA. Architect the settlement layer as provider-agnostic from day one.

2. Fix the OAuth code exchange. The existing Kolab shell has incomplete server-side PKCE code exchange and CSP conflicts. Both are security-critical and must close before multi-tenant work begins — you will be holding contract and payout data for competing managers on one platform.
