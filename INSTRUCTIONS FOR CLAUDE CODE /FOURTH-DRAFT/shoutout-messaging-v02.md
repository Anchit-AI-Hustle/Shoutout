# SHOUTOUT — Messaging
### v02 · the positioning line, rewritten

---

## What was wrong

> ~~The operating system and money rail for the independent music economy. India first, built to be the universal identity layer for artists, managers and organisers worldwide.~~

Four failures, in order of severity:

1. **No person and no situation.** Nothing in that sentence is a thing a human does. Compare: "a producer needs a vocalist." One of those you can picture.
2. **Two stacked metaphors.** "Operating system" *and* "money rail" — the reader has to hold two abstractions before reaching the noun.
3. **"Universal identity layer"** is opaque to everyone including most investors. It describes an internal architectural ambition, not a benefit.
4. **Written for the wrong audience.** It talks to someone who already knows the category. Your supply side — artists, producers, managers — is the audience that decides whether this company works, and it says nothing to them.

**The diagnosis is not "too abstract."** It is *one register, aimed at the smaller audience.* The abstraction is fine — for investors. It should simply never be shown to an artist.

---

## The system: two registers, one product

| | Public register | Investor register |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | Artists, producers, managers, organisers | VCs, labels, brand partners, press |
| **Job** | Make someone want to use it | Make someone understand the category and the moat |
| **Voice** | Concrete, verb-led, situational | Structural, precise, numerate |
| **Where** | App, site, social, SHOUTOUT Stage, all product copy | Deck, memo, data room, partnership decks |

Never mix them. An artist who reads "transaction rail" leaves. An investor who reads only "put out a shout" doesn't understand what they'd be funding.

## PUBLIC REGISTER

### The line
> ## Put out a shout. Someone answers.

Six words. It is the brand name used as a verb, it describes the entire mechanic, and it works identically for a producer looking for a vocalist and a promoter looking for an act. Own this construction relentlessly — consistent proprietary use is also what eventually turns a weak trademark into a defensible one.

### The line, with the payoff attached
> ## Put out a shout. Someone answers.
> **Need a vocalist. Need a gig. Need to get paid. Put out a shout.**

### One sentence
> SHOUTOUT is where musicians find each other, get booked, and actually get paid.

Fifteen words. Three verbs. No metaphor. "Actually" is doing real work — it acknowledges that getting paid is the thing that currently fails.

### The paragraph (site hero, app onboarding)
> A producer needs a vocalist. A manager needs a gig for their artist. A promoter needs an act for Saturday night.
>
> On SHOUTOUT you put out a shout, and people answer. Then the money moves through us — contracts signed, splits agreed before anyone sends a file, everyone paid on time. Including the session players.

Three sentences of situation before a single sentence of product. The situation is what makes someone read the product sentence.

### The manifesto line (unchanged — it already works)
> **You don't need a label. You need a back office.**

Keep this. It is doing a different job: it is the argument, not the description. Use it as a closer, never as an opener.

## INVESTOR REGISTER

The abstraction is allowed here, because the audience is buying the structure. But earn it — lead with the mechanic, then name the category.

### One sentence
> SHOUTOUT is the transaction rail for independent music: collaboration, booking, sponsorship and licensing all run through one object, one match engine and one settlement layer.

### The paragraph
> Every platform in music monetises the artist's attention. None of them work for the artist. SHOUTOUT is the artist's side of the table — the place where a collaborator is found, a gig is booked, a contract is signed and the money is split.
>
> One primitive carries all of it. A Shout is a call that someone answers, and it is the same object whether a producer needs a vocalist or a promoter needs a headliner. That collapses four products into one system, and it produces two assets nobody else in this market holds: a fee index built from every settled booking, and a verified credits graph built from every split sheet signed.
>
> India first — a ₹35,370 cr ($4.16B) artist-fee economy with no infrastructure — and structurally portable to every market where music runs on managers and WhatsApp.

### The category line (for a deck spine or a memo header)
> The back office for independent music.

Five words, and it is the only abstraction worth keeping, because "back office" is a phrase civilians already understand.

## Rules

1. **The verb is the brand.** "Put out a shout." Never "create a listing," never "post a request," never "submit a brief."
2. **Lead with a person in a situation.** Not a category, not an architecture.
3. **Never say "operating system," "money rail," "identity layer" or "ecosystem" to an artist.** Those words belong in the deck and nowhere else.
4. **Never say "AI music generator."** Studio is a communication tool — it helps you show someone the sound in your head.
5. **Say the money part out loud.** "Get paid" and "on time" are the words that convert in this market. Do not soften them into "streamlined settlements."
6. **Name the sidemen.** "Including the session players" is the single most credibility-earning clause in the whole message set, because everyone in this industry knows they get paid last.
7. **Hinglish is allowed and encouraged** in Indian social and in-app copy. Clean global English in investor and brand material.
